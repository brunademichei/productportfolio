Third-Party Credentials For Payroll

A clear, guided setup so customers can register the third-party tax-provider credentials that US payroll depends on, before they run any payroll activity.

Note: this is an external portfolio. The real product screens are internal, so this case is shown as low-fidelity wireframes that communicate the structure and interaction without exposing any confidential data.

My role
UX Designer (interaction & UI)
Product
Enterprise payroll (USA)
Platform
Enterprise web
Focus
IA, interaction design, system patterns

Overview

This is a highly complex enterprise payroll product used to manage payroll operations for large-scale organizations, processing critical employee compensation data under strict compliance and regulatory requirements. Because of its complexity and high-impact nature, the product demands clarity, consistency, and precision in every user interaction.

I work closely with Product Managers, Development Leads, and Engineers to translate complex business requirements and user needs into clear, structured experiences. My role involves interpreting discovery insights and transforming them into user flows, interaction models, and UI aligned with the product's design guidelines and existing patterns. I also take part in Agile ceremonies, bringing usability feedback and creating tickets when I spot usability problems, UI inconsistencies, or unexpected behaviors.

The requirement

This is the requirement I designed for:

The customer must log in and, before performing any activities (such as hiring), register their third-party tax-provider credentials. Payroll depends on this provider for tax calculation, jurisdiction determination, and forms processing, and the product cannot be used until this setup is complete. If a future integration needs credentials, they can be added here too. These settings are metadata-driven, so when a user logs in for the first time, the relevant data is already preconfigured.

The challenge

The credentials step is a hard gate: nothing else in payroll works until it is done. The design had to make a back-office, compliance-heavy task feel discoverable and trustworthy, with clear feedback about what was saved, what was active, and what still needed attention.

The flow I designed

The configuration lives inside the payroll fine-tuning area, reusing patterns customers already know. I designed clear states (Maintained and Configured), an activation toggle per credential version, and an activation log so administrators can trust and trace what happened. Below, the flow is presented as low-fidelity wireframes.

Outcome

The result is a guided, discoverable setup that unblocks first-time logins and gives administrators confidence at every step: a predictable place to configure credentials, explicit status feedback as they move from Maintained to Configured, and an activation log for traceability, all consistent with existing product patterns.
